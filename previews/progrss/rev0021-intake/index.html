02.6 / Continuity of trust

Recovery Circle

Pre-authorize guardians while your key still exists. Later, distinct signatures can help re-establish trust — never without a recipient's consent.

Share exported Courier files manually; live peer connections are deferred in this working edition.

Guardians cannot reconstruct your private key. Recovery transfers an explicitly pinned public trust relationship to a new key; it cannot restore lost observations or your old signature. Recipients must have pre-registered your owner-signed charter. Never accept a newly invented policy as a substitute. Confirm guardians and successor out of band.

① Prepare a recovery charter · BEFORE loss

Each collaborator exports their public Guardian Card, then you paste an array of 2–5 cards. Your CURRENT signing key authorizes the roster, scope, and threshold. Save and send the resulting charter to your followers for advance pinning.

Guardian identities are distinct keys, not one person's repeated signatures. The owner retains no escrow fragments; a charter expires after at most one year.

② Assemble the recovery packet · AFTER loss

Import a previously issued charter. A new signing identity signs a request. Guardians import that packet, independently verify the requester's claim, append their own signed votes, and return it. Import matching packets to combine distinct votes.

You can use an existing staged replacement key for rehearsal, or your new active key in a new browser profile. Guardian votes never replace proof that the old key authorized the charter.

③ Inspect, pre-pin, and explicitly approve

A recipient must pin the charter while the OLD signing key is still trusted. A completed packet cannot create its own trusted charter. Quorum never triggers an automatic update.

A signed tree root commits to approved owner-signed recovery-charter revisions. Compact consistency proofs show a later root extends a pinned older root; inclusion receipts prove a chosen event digest is in the tree. No guardian rosters or feed content are shared.

First pinning requires independent identity confirmation. A signed checkpoint proves control of one key, not a real-world identity or that the publisher revealed every possible history. Subsequent updates MUST carry a consistency proof against YOUR pinned tree root. No automatic trust, subscriptions or polling changes.

⑦ Proof Exchange · request missing history, investigate signed forks

An explicit, offline-capable request/reply protocol built on the Merkle Observatory. Requesters share only two signed public checkpoints. Responders reveal a compact prefix proof, never their charter bodies, watched URLs, private HTML or feed contents.

Two kinds of evidence. Two valid owner signatures on different roots at the same tree size prove signed equivocation. Different-sized heads are unresolved until checked with an append-only consistency proof. Missing proof is not automatically a fork. Sharing a request reveals the public identity and revisions you care about, so exchange only by deliberate action.

Unsigned requests are not authentication. A reply must contain both requested signed heads and a valid consistency path. Nothing updates your pin until you separately approve it.
